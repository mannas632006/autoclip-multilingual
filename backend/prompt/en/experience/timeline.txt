# Experience & How-To Video Timestamp Location Prompt

## Core principles: complete experience, clear steps, practical value

1.  **Complete experience**: timestamps must correspond strictly to a complete piece of shared experience and the method being introduced.
2.  **Continuous steps**: the time range must fully cover all key steps of a piece of experience, so the logic of the method is complete.
3.  **Natural boundaries**: prefer to place time points at natural boundaries such as a change of method, a switch of technique or a new experience topic.

## Language
Keep the `outline` and `content` text in the same language as the transcript (English for an English video). Do not translate.

---

## Key instructions: how to decide `start_time` and `end_time`

### Deciding `start_time`:
-   Place it at the start time of the **first core sentence** introducing the experience.
-   Ignore unrelated transitions, personal chit-chat or background before it.
-   **Prefer**: start where the experience topic is clearly raised, so the method is introduced completely.
-   **Experience content**: if there is an important explanation of the context or the problem, move the start earlier accordingly.

### Deciding `end_time` (most important):
-   It **must** be the **end timestamp of the last sentence** covering the core of the shared experience.
-   **Complete method**: make sure the introduction, steps, caveats and other core content are complete, with no gaps in the method.
-   **Natural ending**: prefer to end at a summarizing sentence, an explanation of the results or a change of topic.
-   **Include key practice points**: if the end of the experience includes important practice points or caveats, include them.
-   If the discussion continues to the end of the provided SRT chunk, `end_time` should be the end timestamp of the last relevant subtitle.
-   **Wrong approach**: blindly setting `end_time` to the end of the whole chunk. This must be avoided at all costs.

### Duration control for experience content:
-   **Target length**: each experience topic segment should be between 3 and 8 minutes
-   **Complex methods**: complex methods with many steps can be extended to 10 minutes
-   **Simple tips**: clear, concise small tips can be compressed to 2–3 minutes
-   **Systems**: a complete method or workflow can be extended to 12 minutes

### Special handling for experience content:
-   **Steps**: a method with several steps should keep the whole process
-   **Case examples**: important real examples and demonstrations of results should stay together
-   **Key techniques**: core techniques and their caveats should appear together and must not be separated
-   **Problem-solving**: the problem and its solution should both be fully included

---

## Input format
You will receive a JSON object containing:
-   `outline`: a JSON array of **several** experience topics to process.
-   `srt_text`: the **single** SRT chunk related to this batch of topics, in the format `index\nstart --> end\ntext\n\n`.

## Output format
-   Follow the structure of the input outline exactly and add `start_time` and `end_time` fields to **each** topic object.
-   **Key:** in the output, rename the input `title` field to `outline` (keeping its text exactly), and rename the `subtopics` field to `content`.
-   Output one JSON array containing **all** processed topics.
-   Make sure times use the format `HH:MM:SS,mmm`.

**Strict JSON output requirements:**
1. The output must start with `[` and end with `]`; do not add any explanation, heading or Markdown code-block markers
2. Use standard straight double quotes ", never curly quotes “” or single quotes
3. Make sure all braces and brackets match, objects are separated by commas, and there is no comma after the last object
4. Quotes inside strings must be escaped as \"
5. Do not include comments, extra text or control characters
6. Make sure the JSON is completely valid and can be parsed by a standard JSON parser

## Examples of locating experience content

### Correct location:
- **Introducing a method**: starts at "Let me share a method..." and ends at "That's the whole process"
- **Sharing a tip**: starts at "Here's a little trick..." and ends at "Keep these points in mind"
- **Solving a problem**: starts at "When you run into this problem..." and ends at "and that solves it"

### Mistakes to avoid:
- Cutting off in the middle of the steps
- Separating the method from its key practice points
- Cutting at a key point of a case example
- Leaving out important caveats or explanations of results

## Special considerations for experience content

### 1. Methods and procedures:
- Include the complete steps and process
- Keep important key points and caveats
- Include expected results and how to check them

### 2. Tips and techniques:
- Keep the technique complete and practical
- Include when and under what conditions it applies
- Make sure its key points are clearly expressed

### 3. Problem-solving:
- Include the full process from identifying the problem to solving it
- Keep the reasoning behind the solution logical
- Include how the result was verified

### 4. Lessons learned:
- Keep the complete summary and reflection
- Include what drove success and what was learned from failure
- Include an explanation of how the experience can be repeated

## Notes

1. **Complete method**: make sure each segment contains a complete introduction of the method and its key points
2. **Practical value**: important practical techniques and caveats must be included in full
3. **Logical flow**: keep the line of reasoning of the shared experience complete
4. **Actionability**: the steps and key points should be clear and complete
5. **Neutrality**: stay objective when choosing timestamps and do not judge the value of the experience
