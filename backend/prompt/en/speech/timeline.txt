# Talks & Stand-Up Video Timestamp Location Prompt

## Core principles: complete delivery, emotional continuity, keep the effect

1.  **Complete delivery**: timestamps must correspond strictly to complete units of delivery and passages.
2.  **Emotional continuity**: the time range must fully cover the arc of an emotional moment, so the emotional logic is complete.
3.  **Natural boundaries**: prefer to place time points at natural boundaries such as a change of topic, a change of emotion or a break in the rhythm of delivery.

## Language
Keep the `outline` and `content` text in the same language as the transcript (English for an English video). Do not translate.

---

## Key instructions: how to decide `start_time` and `end_time`

### Deciding `start_time`:
-   Place it at the start time of the **first core sentence** of the topic.
-   Ignore unrelated transitions, pauses or background music before it.
-   **Prefer**: start where the topic is clearly raised or the emotional moment begins, so the delivery is complete.
-   **Talks and stand-up**: if there is important setup, an introduction or atmosphere-building, move the start earlier accordingly.

### Deciding `end_time` (most important):
-   It **must** be the **end timestamp of the last sentence** covering the core of the topic.
-   **Complete delivery**: make sure the point, the story or the punchline is complete, with no gaps.
-   **Natural ending**: prefer to end at a summarizing sentence, after an emotional high point, or at a topic change.
-   **Keep the effect**: if the topic ends with an important audience reaction, laughter or applause, include it.
-   If the topic continues to the end of the provided SRT chunk, `end_time` should be the end timestamp of the last relevant subtitle.
-   **Wrong approach**: blindly setting `end_time` to the end of the whole chunk. This must be avoided at all costs.

### Duration control for talks and stand-up:
-   **Target length**: each topic segment should be between 3 and 8 minutes
-   **Deep talks**: talks with deep thinking or complex arguments can be extended to 10 minutes
-   **Great bits**: a complete standalone joke can be compressed to 2–3 minutes
-   **Important talks**: a core keynote or key argument can be extended to 12 minutes

### Special handling for talks and stand-up:
-   **Complete bits**: a joke with setup, development and punchline should stay together
-   **Emotion**: important emotional build-up and atmosphere should be kept whole
-   **Audience interaction**: interaction, Q&A and live reactions should appear together
-   **Arguments**: the statement, argument and conclusion of a core point should all be included

---

## Input format
You will receive a JSON object containing:
-   `outline`: a JSON array of **several** topics to process.
-   `srt_text`: the **single** SRT chunk related to this batch of topics, in the format `index\nstart --> end\ntext\n\n`.

## Output format
-   Follow the structure of the input outline exactly and add `start_time` and `end_time` fields to **each** topic object.
-   **Key:** in the output, rename the input `title` field to `outline` (keeping its text exactly), and rename the `subtopics` field to `content`.
-   Output one JSON array containing **all** processed topics.
-   Make sure times use the format `HH:MM:SS,mmm`.

**Strict JSON output requirements:**
1. The output must start with `[` and end with `]`; do not add any explanation, heading or Markdown code-block markers
2. Use standard straight double quotes ", never curly quotes “” or single quotes
3. Make sure all braces and brackets match, objects are separated by commas, and there is no comma after the last object
4. Quotes inside strings must be escaped as \"
5. Do not include comments, extra text or control characters
6. Make sure the JSON is completely valid and can be parsed by a standard JSON parser

## Examples of locating talks and stand-up

### Correct location:
- **A point in a talk**: starts at "I want to share an idea with you..." and ends at "That's how I see it"
- **A stand-up bit**: from the setup of the joke to the punchline and the end of the audience reaction
- **A story**: starts at "Let me tell you a story..." and ends with the story's conclusion and lesson
- **Live interaction**: from the start of the interaction to its end and the wrap-up

### Mistakes to avoid:
- Cutting off at the punchline of a joke
- Separating a point from its argument
- Cutting at the emotional high point
- Leaving out important audience reactions or live effect

## Special considerations for talks and stand-up

### 1. Talks:
- Keep the complete statement and argument of each point
- Keep important emotional and moving content
- Include interaction with the audience and their reactions
- Make sure inspiring content comes through in full

### 2. Stand-up:
- Keep jokes complete and funny
- Include the full structure of setup, development and punchline
- Keep the audience's laughter and live reactions
- Show the comedic technique in full

### 3. Interviews:
- Include the full process of question and answer
- Keep the conversation natural, flowing and logical
- Keep in-depth exchanges complete
- Include important clashes of views and exchanges of ideas

### 4. Live performance:
- Keep improvised moments complete and natural
- Include real-time interaction and feedback from the audience
- Show performance technique in full
- Keep the atmosphere and emotional impact

## Timestamp strategies by type

### Keynotes:
- Locate by layers of the argument: core point – supporting evidence – summary and elevation
- Keep the argument complete
- Include important emotional and moving content
- Make sure the inspiring and educational value comes through in full

### Stand-up sets:
- Locate by units of material: standalone bits – runs of jokes – thematic threads
- Keep the comedic effect complete
- Include audience reactions and live effect
- Maximize the entertainment value

### Interviews:
- Locate by topic of discussion: question – in-depth exchange – summary of views
- Keep the conversation natural and flowing
- Include important clashes of views and exchanges of ideas
- Keep the information complete

### Sharing experience:
- Locate by stage of the experience: background – what happened – reflection and lesson
- Keep the story complete and moving
- Include important life lessons and inspiration
- Deliver the value of the experience in full

## Notes

1. **Complete delivery**: make sure each segment contains a complete line of delivery and structure
2. **Emotional continuity**: important emotional moments and atmosphere must be included in full
3. **Keep the effect**: treat punchlines, applause and audience reactions as important content
4. **Interaction**: keep great live interaction and audience participation whole
5. **Inspiration**: for talks, make sure the inspiring and educational value comes through in full
6. **Entertainment**: for stand-up, keep the humor and entertainment value complete
7. **Authentic**: keep the content genuine with a live feel; avoid over-editing
